// About

MirrorAction mirrors a character's animation in Maya: one arm, a whole pose, a stretch of animation or the entire take. It is laid out like Biped's Copy/Paste in 3ds Max, but works on any rig - a character on bare joints or one on IK controls. You show the tool once where the character's left and right are, and after that mirroring is a button on a narrow panel that stays open next to the viewport.

Copy and paste, on either side

Three modes decide what a copy is. Posture takes the selected parts on the current frame: this hand onto the other arm. Pose takes the whole character on the frame. Track takes the selection over a range of frames: this step onto the other side. Paste puts a copy back where it came from, Paste opposite puts it mirrored. Every copy gets a picture, shot close up from the viewport's angle, and the copies sit in collections that the tool keeps by itself between Maya sessions. Save collection packs one into a single file with its pictures, ready to hand to a colleague.

Swap and Mirror animation

Swap trades the animation of the left and right arm or leg, each one mirrored, and it trades exactly what you selected: nothing is added behind your back. Mirror animation mirrors the whole take in one click: a walk that starts on the right foot now starts on the left and heads the other way. It asks before it starts.

Set up once, per character

The setup is three steps: the root of the skeleton, a frame where the character stands symmetrically, and what to mirror. Find looks for a symmetric frame in the take; if the character never stops, the tool keys the bind pose on a free frame before the take, and the animation stays as it was. You select the left-side joints and the right-side pairs are found by name. Then Save, and the next scene with this character picks the preset up by itself. The tool measures every pair on the rest pose instead of guessing from axis names, so rigs with non-zero channels are fine too.

A character on controls

For a rigged character there is a stick figure instead of a joint list: a ball for each arm and leg, Hips for the pelvis and Spine for the spine, neck and head. Select the controls of one side, click the ball, done. The preset remembers the controls' T-pose and keys it on a free frame in the next scene. + Arms and + Legs add another pair of limbs - four arms, a horse, a spider. Clavicles, fingers and toes keyed by hand stay on the joints page, and both pages work together.

Cycle: the other leg, half a cycle later

The right leg of the walk is done. The left one is the same leg, mirrored and half a cycle later. Type in one cycle, press ½ and Mirror with offset: the other leg is built over the whole playback range, on an animation layer of its own. Do not like it - delete the layer; like it - merge it down. IK feet are placed along the line the character travels, so the sway of the pelvis does not make them slide.

Anchors without frame numbers

An anchor holds an IK foot or hand in place while it is planted. Press its button on the frame where the foot lands, press again where it may go - the two presses are the range. Ease out sets how many frames the limb takes to return to the animation. The holds live on animation layers and stay editable; Bake turns them into ordinary keys at the end of the shot. The buttons come from the stick figure by themselves and are stored in the scene.

No symmetric pose at all

Some characters have neither a bind pose nor a single symmetric frame. Then you set one side by hand - a T-pose, an A-pose - and the Symmetry tab builds the other one. Only rotations change; bone lengths stay as the rigger set them, and one undo puts everything back.

What it does not do

  • it does not guess where left and right are: the character needs a symmetric rest pose, found, keyed from the bind pose, or built on the Symmetry tab;
  • it does not add parts to Swap by itself: select both sides, and on an IK rig the whole arm with its pole;
  • it does not hold bare joints with anchors: anchors need IK hands and feet;
  • it does not overwrite a frame taken by animation with the T-pose from a preset;
  • it does not delete your copies and collections: no limits, no expiry, only you delete them;
  • it never goes online.
