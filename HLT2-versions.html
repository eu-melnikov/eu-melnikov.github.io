// About

HLT 2 (High Level Tracker 2) shows where the working day went and lets you answer "how is it going?" in ten seconds. It works hand in hand with Jira and Telegram: you log time once, in Jira, as you always do, and your worklogs, meetings, notes and review files arrive in the journal on their own. You only type in what lives in neither place: a hallway conversation, a promise, an idea. Whatever is left open piles up in Tails instead of getting lost.

Your Jira time comes in by itself

Every worklog of yours becomes a task record on its day: the issue key and title, the start time, the minutes logged, the worklog comment and a link to the issue. The record is already marked done - the work happened, so it never hangs around as a tail.

  • Nothing is typed twice: log 1h 30m on an issue and the record shows up on that day.
  • Changes in Jira follow: edit a worklog and the record updates, delete it and the record is cancelled and stops counting.
  • Your own edits to a record stay until Jira changes that same field.
  • The issue key opens the issue's history - every day you worked on it, with the total time.
  • Time is filed into the right project of your journal by itself: wherever that Jira project's keys already live, or into the default project.

The token is entered once and kept in the Windows Credential Manager. Sync runs in the background, every 15 minutes by default, and on F5.

Telegram fills the journal for you

HLT 2 reads Telegram under your own account, the way a regular client does, and turns the conversation into records:

  • Meetings from a calendar bot land on their day with time and duration. The bot deletes a meeting and the record is cancelled; it moves one and the record follows.
  • Inbox channel: forward any message to your own channel and a note appears with its text and a link back to the original.
  • Files in chats that match the review masks (.mb, .ma and .fbx by default) become review records on the day and at the time they were sent. A mask can be added later too - .zip, say: the chats are read again over the last 30 days, and reviews you deleted do not come back.

The Telegram sign-in and keys are stored next to the program, encrypted by Windows (DPAPI) for your account.

Overview: "how is it going?" answered

The screen for the morning and the minute before a call. On the left: what is in progress, what not to forget, and what is coming up. Below: where the day went, every record with a bar of its minutes. The last three days with records are ready as text - Copy puts a day on the clipboard for a chat or a stand-up, Copy the week takes the whole week with its total. A line reads like this: "✓ GLM-168 Polish Wren's attacks after game design feedback".

Further down is the month in hours: time recorded against working time, the share eaten by meetings, how many weekdays have no records at all, and a chart by day.

Work: the day by the hour, and the week

The chosen day on an hour scale, in lanes: tasks, reviews, meetings, notes. Drag a block to another time, pull its right edge to change the duration, Ctrl+wheel to zoom into the hours. Holes - working time with nothing recorded - are tinted red, and a double-click in one adds a record that fills it exactly.

Underneath is the whole week, each record a chip; drop a chip into another column and the record moves to that day. Click a record and the panel on the right shows everything about it, its links and the issue history.

A report for the week and the month

The third screen is Report: week + month, key 3. A week collects small work and serious work alike; only what you pick goes in here. Right-click a record → "Add to report…" or press R, and add a comment if you like: what was done and why it matters.

  • Week - the picked records by day, with their comments.
  • Month - the same picks grouped by week; the arrows step through months.
  • Copy - the report as text, ready for a chat, Jira or an e-mail.
  • Save .docx or .txt - as a file.

Comments can be edited on any day, and a Jira or Telegram sync never overwrites them.

Tails, rescheduling and reminders

Tails is everything still open, except meetings, in one list: search by words, filter by kind and age, close a record or take it into today's work, close everything found in one go.

Postpone to carries a record over to another day. It is smart about time: if hours were already spent, they stay on the day they were spent, and an open continuation moves to the new day.

Remind me in goes from a week to a year, with the date shown right in the menu. A reminder waits under Ahead, and on its day it pops up as a message from the tray.

Lives in the tray, travels with its folder

The close button hides the window in the tray while the program keeps syncing and reminding. One tick in Settings starts HLT 2 with Windows, straight into the tray. Launching it again does not open a second copy - it brings up the window that is already running. Journals (.hlt2) show the program's icon in Explorer, and a double click opens one, even while HLT 2 sits in the tray.

The journal is a single .hlt2 file wherever you created it. It saves itself after every edit, and the previous version is set aside as a backup - the last twenty sit next to the journal. Settings live in settings.json next to the program and move with its folder.

Help → Check for updates opens the page with the versions of HLT 2. There is one program folder for every version - HLT2: to update, delete everything in it except settings.json and secrets, and unzip the new archive into the same place - your settings and the Telegram sign-in stay.

A tutorial and a tutorial project

You can start without connecting anything of your own. Help → Tutorial project Glowmire opens a made-up journal: two months of an animator's work on an invented game - dailies and planning, tasks with keys, review files, notes, rescheduled work, reminders and report marks. Its last day is always today, so every screen looks alive: click, drag, close tails - nothing real is touched. Jira and Telegram write nothing into it.

Help → Tutorial for beginners is a step-by-step lesson with pictures and numbered callouts for every button: unzip and run, walk through the screens of the tutorial project with small exercises, create your own journal, make a Jira token, get the keys at my.telegram.org, sign in to Telegram and live through your first working day. The tutorial comes in English and Russian, and the tutorial project itself lies in the demo folder next to the program and opens with a double click.

What it does not do

  • It does not go online on its own. It talks only to your Jira and to Telegram, and only if you connect them. Without them HLT 2 is a plain manual journal.
  • It does not log time to Jira. It only reads your worklogs; colleagues' worklogs never reach your journal.
  • It does not fit every Jira. It is built for self-hosted Jira (Server / Data Center) with a personal access token.
  • It does not understand every calendar bot. Meetings are read from one message format; other bots' messages are skipped.
  • It cannot do without your Telegram account. You need an api_id and api_hash from my.telegram.org - issued once, free of charge.
  • It does not carry secrets across. On another computer or another Windows user you sign in to Telegram and enter the Jira token again - by design.
  • It does not download files. Pictures and documents stay in Telegram; the journal keeps a link.
  • There is no Ctrl+Z. That is why deleting a record asks first, and why backups of the journal sit next to it.
  • It is not code-signed. On first launch Windows may warn about an unknown publisher.

2.0.2 2026-10-26 LATEST 57 Mb

2.0.2 is a small update to the Work screen: a busy day no longer stretches the page, clips carry no long names at their side, and the pop-up tooltips are gone.

Changed

  • The theses of the day scroll. On a day with many tasks done, the list on the right no longer grows into a long sheet that pushes the week down: the card stays short and the rest scrolls inside it.
  • No names beside the clips on the day track. A task's name is written only inside its clip and cut with "…" when it does not fit. The full name is on the right panel when the clip is picked.
  • No pop-up tooltips. The long tooltips over clips, buttons and fields are gone: everything they said is in the manual (F1). The only one left shows where a record lands while you drag it.

Installing and updating

  • Quit the program, delete everything in the HLT2 folder except settings.json and secrets, and unzip the new archive into the same place. Journals, settings and the Telegram sign-in stay.
2.0.1 2026-10-24 57 Mb

2.0.1 is a small bug-fix update: files under a newly added review mask are now found in older messages too, a long name on the day track is no longer written twice, and the Help menu can check for updates.

Fixed

  • Files from chats under a new mask. A review mask added later (.zip, say) used to catch only files sent after it: whatever came before had already been read past. Now a new mask makes the chats be read again over the last 30 days. From what was read before, only files under the new mask are taken, so reviews you deleted do not come back.
  • A long name on the day track. When a name did not fit its clip, it was written a second time to the right of it - and showed up twice. Now only really narrow clips are labelled at the side; everywhere else the name is cut with "…", and the full one is in the tooltip.

New

  • Help → Check for updates opens the page with the versions of HLT 2 in the browser.

Installing and updating

  • The program folder is now one for every version - HLT2, with no number; the number is only in the name of the archive. To update, quit the program, delete everything in the folder except settings.json and secrets, and unzip the new archive into the same place.
  • Coming from 2.0.0 (folder HLT2-2.0.0): move settings.json and secrets into the new HLT2 folder once. If starting with Windows was on, the new HLT2.exe points Windows at itself.
  • The manual and the README cover this in their section on updating.
